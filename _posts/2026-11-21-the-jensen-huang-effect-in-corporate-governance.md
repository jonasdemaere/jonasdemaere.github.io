## What The Board Decided

The recent developments in the aftermath of the dismissal of Jensen Huang as President, CEO, and member of the board of directors of NVIDIA may have a lasting effect on corporate governance structures in founder-led companies.

Founders, in particular tech founders, have been astonished these past two weeks by the decision NVIDIA's board of directors has made in the aftermath of the news of November 11, 2026, around Jensen Huang. The narrative is that they view the recent dismissal as unfair and out of proportion in relation to the impact Huang has had as founder and CEO on the success of NVIDIA in the past three decades.

Notably, the independent directors of NVIDIA's board get a lot of scrutiny by the former mentioned community. Many wonder how directors such as a former CEO of Pepsi-Cola North America or a former president of Taco Bell International, career corporate executives, can make informed decisions about the future of NVIDIA. In a traditional corporate governance sense, this is the role these independent directors are intended to play. They must hold the executives accountable for their actions in relation to the shareholders' interests. I expect founder-led technology companies to move away from that model over the next decade.

## Toward A Shift in Board Capital

I argue that the profiles in the board of directors of founder-led firms, especially in technology, are about to change. I expect board capital (the set of human capital and social capital in a board) to change over the next decade. I will call this the Jensen Huang Effect.

A large number of stakeholders (shareholders, employees, investors, customers, suppliers, government representatives) of NVIDIA have been vocal these past two weeks that they do not agree with the decision the board of directors has made. Although the board acted as you would expect a typical board to act, the question becomes whether the stakeholders are aligned with this decision.

My thesis is that founder-led companies will see a change in director profiles and, as a result, in board capital over the next decade. I foresee that founders are going to compose their board with directors that are more like them, i.e., entrepreneurial, visionary, decisive, and innovative. I project that there will be fewer corporate profiles, but more founder-like profiles in the board of these companies. Founders are going to think twice about who to install on their boards, and stakeholders will accept this. The key argument is that the founder's continuity itself is a source of value. Stakeholders will expect the board to weigh that value against a founder's personal conduct. That trade-off has limits, but the composition of the board of directors could determine whether a founder stays or goes. I expect that in founder-led companies the Jensen Huang Effect is going to have a lasting impact on corporate governance.
